# Ideas mined from Daniel's 5 papers for the RH / prime-gas program (2026-10-14)
(Per Daniel: idea mining, not an editorial fix list. RH is OPEN. Numbers are mpmath 30 dps floats, not certified.)

1. P(λ)=πλ/sinh(πλ) IS the Kubo–Mori (Fisher) kernel κ_β(ω)=(βω/2)/sinh(βω/2) at β=2π. Exact identity (diff 0.0). The thermality paper's weight and the Fisher paper's Hessian kernel are the same function. THEOREM, elementary.
2. The archimedean Weil term is a Lévy–Khintchine exponent. Re ψ(1/4+ir/2) − ψ(1/4) = ∫_0^∞ (1−cos ru) ν(u) du, with ν(u) = 2e^{−u/2}/(1−e^{−2u}) = e^{u/2}/sinh u, that is, 2e^{u/2} × [the papers' jump density 1/(2 sinh u)]. Checked at r = 1, 5, 14 (error ≤ 2e−22). CLASSICAL (Gauss's integral for ψ), but it's the precise way the Lévy generator enters the gamma term.
3. One jump measure. In the explicit formula, the gamma-factor part and the primes come in with the SAME sign: g is integrated against M = ν(u)du + 2Σ Λ(n) n^{−1/2} δ_{log n}, which is a positive measure. Only the archimedean part has the g(0) compensator, and the pole term e^{±u/2} sits opposite. Gaussian test (σ = 0.5) against the first 39 zeros: diff 1.9e−25. NUMERICAL. RH (Weil positivity) then reads: the pole (trivial-character) gain dominates one positive Lévy jump law, continuous at ∞ and atomic at the primes. This matches the Fisher paper's "break-even" picture.
4. Bost–Connes β=1 ↔ BW β=2π. The prime frequencies are log n at β=1, the boost frequencies λ at β=2π. The KM norm of the ladder at β=1 is n^{−1/2}/κ_1(log n), and for a prime this equals Λ-weighted (1−1/p)/log p. So the prime gas's Fisher metric is the geometric-mean metric times 1/κ. Lean ZetaFisherMetric could host κ_β as the bridge. CONJECTURE/lead.
5. Volume / trivial representation ↔ pole. The double zero of the Plancherel weight at λ=0, and the trivial rep that entropy can't weigh, correspond to the trivial character, the pole of ζ, which the gas boils at s=1. Λ is a Lagrange multiplier, and the residue is fixed by counting. Structural analogy only.
6. Unitarity cuts / dispersion (Loops v19). A cut-to-loop reconstruction is a Kramers–Kronig statement, and the analogue is reconstructing ξ'/ξ from its discontinuity. Not tested; the step scripts in ../unpack18 are absent.
